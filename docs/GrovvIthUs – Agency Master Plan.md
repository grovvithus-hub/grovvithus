# GrovvIthUs – Agency Master Plan

Oct 15, 2026 · @sandeepkumar

## 1. Executive summary

GrovvIthUs is a full-service digital agency that builds, markets and maintains a client's entire online presence, from the website and app to the logo, videos and search rankings, under one roof. This plan covers the business, the website, the admin panel, the tech stack, hosting costs, security and legal pages needed to launch.

**Assumptions (change if wrong):** the agency is based in India, the website shows no prices, only pricing models, while internal costs are shown in INR with USD in brackets (approx. ₹85 = $1), and the first version is run by a founder plus 1 to 3 freelancers.

### Who we serve

- **Startups and small businesses** that need a website, logo and basic marketing in one package.
- **Local businesses** (clinics, salons, schools, restaurants, real estate) that need Google Maps ranking, WhatsApp leads and a simple site.
- **Online stores** on Shopify or WooCommerce that need setup, ads and SEO.
- **Creators and personal brands** that need videos, posters and a portfolio site.
- **Growing companies** that need custom software, mobile apps and ongoing maintenance.

### Positioning

Three things set the agency apart from a freelancer and from a big agency:

1. **One team for everything.** The client does not have to hire a developer, designer, editor and marketer separately.
2. **Clear pricing models, not public price tags** (fixed package, monthly retainer, milestones) are explained on the site, and the exact figure comes in a personal quotation.
3. **Monthly retainers** (maintenance, SEO, social media) that give predictable income to the agency and predictable results to the client.

### Business goals for year one

- Launch the website and 6 to 10 portfolio pieces within 60 days (use own brand, free pilot projects and concept work).
- Sign 5 paying clients in the first 90 days and 3 monthly retainers in the first 6 months.
- Rank for "web development agency in \<city>" and similar local searches within 6 months.

### How each service runs

**Web development, WordPress, Shopify**

- Deliverables: sitemap, wireframe, design, build, mobile and speed checks, forms, analytics, 30 days of free fixes.
- Process: discovery call, quote, 50% advance, design approval, build, review (2 revision rounds), launch, handover.
- Tools: Figma, Next.js, WordPress, Shopify, Google Analytics 4, Search Console.

**Custom software and apps**

- Deliverables: requirement document, UI design, working software in 2-week sprints, testing, deployment, source code handover (on final payment).
- Process: discovery paid workshop, fixed-scope milestones, weekly demo, UAT, go-live, 60 days of support.
- Tools: Next.js, Node.js, PostgreSQL/MySQL, React Native, GitHub, Docker.

**Application maintenance**

- Three monthly plans: Basic (updates, backups, uptime monitoring), Standard (plus 3 hours of changes, security scans), Premium (plus 8 hours, priority support, monthly report). Fees are given in the quotation.
- Service levels: first reply within 24 hours (Basic), 8 hours (Standard), 4 hours (Premium).

**Digital marketing, SEO, AEO, GEO, brand promotion**

- Deliverables: monthly plan, content calendar, campaign setup, weekly or monthly report with leads, cost per lead and ranking changes.
- Rule: ad spend is paid directly by the client to Google/Meta; the agency charges a management fee only.
- Be honest in contracts: no guaranteed rankings, only agreed activities and targets.

**Video editing, posters, logos, content**

- Deliverables: source files plus exports; 2 revisions included; delivery time stated per item (reel 24 to 48 hours, logo 5 to 7 days).
- Ownership: the client owns the final files after full payment; the agency may show the work in its portfolio unless the client opts out in writing.

### Quotations, rate card and demo bookings

The public site never shows prices. A visitor picks one of the options below, and the team answers with a personal quotation prepared in the admin panel.

| Visitor option | What the visitor does | What the admin sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Request a quote | Fills the quote form: services, project details, timeline, files, optional budget range | A lead with status New and a **Prepare quote** button |
| Book a demo | Picks a meeting type and a free time slot from live availability | A booking with calendar invite, meeting link and reminders |
| Discuss on a call | Chooses a callback time, or taps call or WhatsApp | A callback task assigned to Sales |
| Not sure yet | Sends a short "tell us your goal" message | A lead tagged *needs discovery* |

**Rate card (private).** Super Admin keeps one internal price list: each item has a service, unit, base rate, pricing model and tax code. Only Super Admin and Sales can see it; it is never sent to the public site.

**Quotation builder.**

- Start from a lead or client; add line items from the rate card (fixed package, milestone, monthly retainer, per item, hourly, ad-management fee) with quantity, unit and notes.
- Add discount with a reason, GST (18% by default), currency (INR or USD), validity date, payment terms (advance and milestone split) and assumptions and exclusions.
- Attach the Terms and Conditions and a Statement of Work draft from templates.
- A discount above a set percentage needs Super Admin approval before the quote can be sent.
- Output is a branded PDF plus a private web link (`/q/<random token>`, expires on the validity date) sent by email or WhatsApp.
- The client can **Accept** (typed name, date, IP address and time logged), **Request changes** (comment), or **Book a call** from the same page; the team sees when the link was opened.
- Every revision is kept as a numbered version; only the latest is valid.
- Automatic reminder to the client before the quote expires, and an alert to the team when its status changes.
- An accepted quote converts in one click into a project, an SOW draft and a request for the advance invoice.
- Reports: quotes sent, accepted rate, time to accept, and results by service.

| Quote status | Meaning | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | Being prepared | Finish and submit |
| Pending approval | Discount needs Super Admin sign-off | Approve or edit |
| Sent | Delivered to the client | Wait; reminder is scheduled |
| Viewed | Client opened the link | Follow up by call |
| Changes requested | Client commented | Create the next version |
| Accepted | Client accepted online | Convert to project |
| Declined or Expired | Closed without a deal | Log the reason |

**Demo and call booking.**

- Meeting types: discovery call, product demo, project review (length and description editable).
- Settings: working hours, holidays, buffer between meetings, notice period, and which team member takes which type (or round robin).
- Two-way sync with Google Calendar; a Google Meet or Zoom link is added automatically.
- Visitor's timezone is detected; confirmation and reminder messages go by email (WhatsApp optional) with reschedule and cancel links.
- Booking statuses: Booked, Completed, No-show, Cancelled; a completed demo can be turned into a quote in one click.
- To save build time in the first release, use a Cal.com embed and add the built-in booking module later; the quote and lead flow stays in the admin panel either way.

## 5. Tech stack decision

Build one Next.js (TypeScript) application that serves both the public website and the admin panel, backed by MySQL, and host it on Hostinger's Node.js hosting. This keeps one codebase, one deployment and one bill, and it is the same stack the agency will sell to clients, so the site itself proves the skill.

### Chosen stack

| Layer | Choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framework | **Next.js 15 (App Router) + React + TypeScript** | Server rendering and static generation give fast pages and strong SEO; one codebase for site, admin and API |
| Styling | **Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui** | Fast to build, consistent design, accessible components, easy to theme to brand colours |
| Animation | **Framer Motion** | Smooth, light animations |
| Database | **MySQL 8** (Hostinger includes it) | Runs on the same server, no extra cost, and fully supported on Hostinger plans |
| ORM | **Prisma** | Typed queries, migrations, simple schema file |
| Authentication | **Auth.js (NextAuth) with credentials + optional Google login, plus 2FA (TOTP)** | Proven, session cookies, role checks in middleware |
| Validation | **Zod** | Same rules on form and server |
| Rich text editor | **TipTap** | Blog and page editing, outputs clean HTML/JSON |
| File storage | **Cloudflare R2** (free 10 GB, no egress fees) with `sharp` for WebP | Keeps uploads safe when the app is redeployed; fast CDN delivery |
| Email | **Hostinger business email (SMTP) via Nodemailer**, or Resend free tier (3,000 emails/month) | Lead notifications and auto-replies |
| Spam protection | **Cloudflare Turnstile** (free) + rate limiting | Stops bots on forms without annoying captchas |
| Analytics | **GA4, Microsoft Clarity, Search Console** | Free and standard |
| Monitoring | **UptimeRobot** (free) + **Sentry** free tier | Alerts if the site goes down or errors |
| DNS, CDN, WAF | **Cloudflare free plan** | DDoS protection, caching, firewall, free SSL |
| Version control and CI | **GitHub** (private repo) + GitHub Actions | Backup of code, automatic build and deploy |
| Design | **Figma** (free tier) | Mockups before coding |
| Project management | **Notion / ClickUp** for the team, **Zoho Books or Razorpay** for invoicing | Run the agency itself |

### Alternatives considered

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WordPress + custom theme | Rejected for the main site | Easy for content but weaker for a custom admin and an agency that sells custom code; more plugin security risk |
| Headless CMS (Strapi, Payload, Sanity) | Possible later | Adds a second service to host and maintain; a custom admin is simpler for this scope |
| MERN (MongoDB) | Rejected | MySQL is included in hosting; MongoDB would need a paid external service |
| Firebase / Supabase | Rejected for v1 | Extra vendor, and data would sit outside the Hostinger server |
| Pure static site (Astro) + no admin | Rejected | You explicitly need an admin panel for projects |
| Laravel (PHP) | Good fallback | Runs natively on Hostinger shared hosting; choose this if the team knows PHP better than JavaScript |

### Rule for choosing the stack for clients

- Brochure site, blog, small business: WordPress.
- Store: Shopify (or WooCommerce if the client wants no monthly fee).
- Custom web app or SaaS: Next.js + PostgreSQL/MySQL.
- Mobile apps: React Native (one code for Android and iOS) or Flutter.

## 6. System architecture

Every visitor and admin request passes through Cloudflare, then reaches one Next.js app on Hostinger that reads from MySQL on the same server and stores images in Cloudflare R2.

&#91;embedded content: architecture · 7 components\]

Cloudflare filters traffic; the app, database and email run on Hostinger; images live in R2; GitHub deploys the code.

### Request flow

1. A visitor opens `grovvithus.com`; Cloudflare answers from cache or forwards to Hostinger.
2. Public pages are pre-built (static generation) and refreshed when the admin publishes (on-demand revalidation), so they load fast and rank well.
3. Forms post to API routes that validate with Zod, check Turnstile and rate limits, save to MySQL, and send an email to the team.
4. Admin users sign in at `/admin`, receive an HTTP-only session cookie, and pass a role check on every page and every API call.
5. Uploaded images go to R2; the database stores only the file address and alt text.

### Deployment pipeline

1. Developer pushes code to a GitHub branch; the pull request runs lint, type check and tests.
2. Merge to `main` triggers GitHub Actions to build and deploy to Hostinger (Node.js app, Git deploy).
3. Environment variables (database, auth secret, R2 keys, SMTP) are set in the Hostinger panel, never in the repository.
4. Prisma migrations run on deploy; a database backup runs first.
5. A staging subdomain (`staging.grovvithus.com`, password protected) is used for testing before production.

## 8. Security instructions

An agency that sells websites is a target, and one hacked client site damages the brand. Treat the checklist below as launch blockers: nothing goes live until every item marked **Must** is done.

### Accounts and access

- [ ] **Must:** Two-factor authentication (authenticator app, not SMS) on Hostinger, domain registrar, Cloudflare, GitHub, Google, social accounts and the admin panel.
- [ ] **Must:** A password manager (Bitwarden free or 1Password) for every credential; passwords of 16+ random characters; no sharing by chat or email.
- [ ] **Must:** Separate accounts for each person; nobody uses the founder's login. Remove access on the day someone leaves.
- [ ] **Must:** Two recovery paths for the main email and domain accounts (backup codes stored offline, a second trusted person).
- [ ] Use a dedicated company email for all admin accounts, not personal inboxes.

### Application security (Next.js admin and API)

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Weak login | Passwords hashed with Argon2id (or bcrypt cost 12+); minimum 12 characters; TOTP 2FA required for admin roles; lock after 5 failed attempts for 15 minutes; generic error text ("email or password is wrong") |
| Stolen session | HTTP-only, Secure, SameSite=Lax cookies; session expiry of 8 hours idle; rotate session on login; "log out everywhere" button |
| Unauthorised access | Role check on every admin page and every API route on the server (never only in the UI); deny by default |
| SQL injection | Prisma parameterised queries only; no raw SQL with user input |
| Cross-site scripting (XSS) | React escapes output by default; sanitise rich text with DOMPurify before saving and before showing; strict Content-Security-Policy header |
| Cross-site request forgery (CSRF) | SameSite cookies, CSRF token on state-changing requests, check `Origin` header |
| Bad file uploads | Allow only image/video/PDF types, check magic bytes not just the extension, max size (10 MB images, 100 MB video), rename files randomly, store in R2 (not on the app server), never execute uploads |
| Spam and bots | Cloudflare Turnstile on public forms, rate limit (for example 5 submissions per IP per hour), honeypot field |
| Brute force and abuse | Rate limit login and API routes (Cloudflare rules plus app-level limiter); log failed attempts |
| Information leaks | No stack traces to users; `.env` never committed; separate secrets for staging and production; remove `X-Powered-By` |
| Vulnerable packages | `npm audit` and Dependabot weekly; pin versions; review before upgrading major versions |
| Insecure direct references | Check that the record belongs to the user's allowed scope before returning it |
| Admin discovery | `/admin` set to `noindex`, optional IP allow-list or Cloudflare Access for Super Admin pages |

### Security headers (set in `middleware.ts` or `next.config.js`)

- `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains`
- `Content-Security-Policy` allowing only your own domain, Cloudflare Turnstile, GA4 and the image CDN
- `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`
- `X-Frame-Options: DENY` (or CSP `frame-ancestors 'none'`)
- `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`
- `Permissions-Policy` disabling camera, microphone and geolocation
- Test at securityheaders.com and aim for an A grade.

### Server, database and network

- [ ] **Must:** HTTPS everywhere, Cloudflare SSL mode Full (strict), automatic HTTP to HTTPS redirect, HSTS.
- [ ] **Must:** Database user with only the rights the app needs; database reachable only from `127.0.0.1`; remote access in hPanel left off.
- [ ] **Must:** Secrets only in Hostinger environment variables; rotate them every 6 months and after any suspected leak.
- [ ] Cloudflare WAF managed rules on; "Under Attack" mode known for emergencies; bot fight mode on.
- [ ] Block direct access to hidden files (`.env`, `.git`) and keep the repository private.
- [ ] If you later use a VPS: SSH keys only, disable password login and root login, firewall allowing 22/80/443 only, Fail2ban, automatic security updates.

### Backups and recovery

| What | How often | Where | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hostinger automatic backups | Daily (Cloud plan) | Hostinger | Restore test every quarter |
| MySQL dump | Daily, keep 14 days and 4 weekly copies | Private cloud storage (not the same server) | Restore to staging monthly |
| Code | Every push | GitHub private repo | Clone and build monthly |
| Media | Continuous | R2 with versioning on | Spot check |
| Secrets and recovery codes | When changed | Password manager plus offline copy | Yearly |

Target: restore the site within 4 hours (recovery time) and lose at most 24 hours of data (recovery point). Write a one-page incident plan: who to call, how to take the site offline, how to restore, and how to inform affected clients.

### Monitoring

- UptimeRobot checks the home page and `/admin/login` every 5 minutes with email and WhatsApp alerts.
- Sentry for application errors; weekly review of failed logins and the audit log.
- Google Search Console security issues report and Cloudflare security events checked weekly.

### Working securely with client data

- Store client files, credentials and invoices in an access-controlled drive; share with expiring links.
- Ask clients to add the agency as a user on their own accounts (Hostinger, Shopify, Google Ads, Meta) instead of sending passwords.
- Sign an NDA when the client asks; keep a register of what personal data you hold and delete it when the project ends and the retention period passes.
- Use licensed software only; track stock images, fonts, music and plugins licences so the agency never delivers pirated assets.
- Train every team member on phishing: verify payment-detail changes by phone, never open unexpected attachments.

## 10. SEO, AEO and GEO plan for the agency site

An agency that sells SEO, AEO and GEO must rank and get cited itself, so the website is built search-first and doubles as a live case study. The plan has three layers: classic search (SEO), direct answers (AEO) and AI-engine citations (GEO).

### Layer 1: SEO (rank in Google and Bing)

| Area | Actions |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | Server-rendered pages, Lighthouse 90+, Core Web Vitals in the green (LCP under 2.5 s, INP under 200 ms, CLS under 0.1), clean URLs, canonical tags, XML sitemap, `robots.txt`, HTTPS, mobile-first, no broken links, 301 redirects from the admin SEO manager |
| On-page | One H1 per page, title under 60 characters, description under 155, descriptive image alt text, internal links between services, portfolio and blog |
| Content | One page per service (17), one page per city if you serve several, a case study per project, 2 to 4 blog posts per month |
| Local | Google Business Profile with category "Web designer" or "Marketing agency", consistent name/address/phone, reviews, local service pages |
| Authority | Listings (Clutch, GoodFirms, Sortlist, LinkedIn, Behance, Dribbble), guest posts, client credit links in the footer of delivered sites (with permission), digital PR |
| Tracking | Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verified on day one; GA4 events for form submits, WhatsApp clicks and calls |

First keyword targets (check volume and difficulty with free tools before committing): "web development company in \[city\]", "website design cost in India", "Shopify developer India", "WordPress maintenance services", "SEO services for small business", "mobile app development company", "video editing services for YouTube", "logo design for startups".

### Layer 2: AEO (be the direct answer)

- Write each page's first 40 to 60 words as a plain answer to a question ("How much does a business website cost in India? Business website pricing depends on the number of pages, features and integrations, and we give an exact figure in a free quotation.").
- Use question headings, short paragraphs, tables and numbered steps so answers can be lifted cleanly.
- Add a FAQ section on every service page with 5 to 8 real questions from sales calls, marked up with FAQPage schema (note: Google shows FAQ rich results only for a limited set of sites, but the markup still helps machines read the page).
- Add schema markup: Organization, LocalBusiness/ProfessionalService, Service, Article, BreadcrumbList, Review (only real reviews), and VideoObject.
- Target featured snippets and "People also ask" boxes by answering related questions in the same article.
- Optimise for voice search with natural-language, conversational question phrases.

### Layer 3: GEO (be cited by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and AI Overviews)

- Let AI crawlers in: do not block `GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Google-Extended` in `robots.txt`, unless you decide otherwise on purpose. Keep important content in server-rendered HTML, not behind scripts.
- Build a clear entity: the same agency name, description, founder, address and services on the website, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, Crunchbase, directories and social profiles. Add `sameAs` links in Organization schema.
- Publish original, quotable material AI engines like to cite: guides on how pricing works and what drives cost (without publishing your rate card), case studies with measured results, comparison articles ("WordPress vs Shopify for small stores"), checklists, and benchmarks from your own work.
- Show expertise: named authors with bios, dates and "last updated", cited sources, and consistent facts.
